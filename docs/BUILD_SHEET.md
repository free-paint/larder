# LARDER: Build Sheet

The wedge is getting back money that was invoiced but never delivered, sold to finance at multi-site groups. Anything that doesn't serve that, or that depends on it working first, waits.

## Now

| # | Item | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | Invoice vs delivery-note matching | This is the core of the CFO's offer and the only thing the evidence pulls toward (C1, C6, C7). It produces the €-found number. |
| 6 | Credit request, generated and tracked | A drafted email per supplier, which **we** send at first. Without it we only find money. The riskiest assumption sits here: does sending one actually get a credit? |

## Next (once the one-site pilot shows recovery is real)

| # | Item | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | Delivery-note capture (built) | Runs by hand for now (photo to WhatsApp, we key it). We build it only after we've seen what receiving staff will actually do at 7am. Building first would mean guessing at the kitchen. |
| 12 | Accounting hand-off | The CFO's hard requirement before nine sites. Start with an export in Merit/e-arveldaja import format. The native API stays **Not now** until 3+ customers use the same system. |
| 9 | Price change alerts | Comes almost free once invoice lines are structured. It tests whether price creep is part of the leakage (C2 switched suppliers twice over prices). |

## Not now (blocked, with trigger)

| # | Item | Blocked on | Promote when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | Multi-site spend dashboard | Needs invoice data flowing for every site in a group | The 9-site pilot is live and C3's group names a price for it. It's then mostly a view over data we already hold. |
| 8 | Over-ordering alerts vs covers | Needs covers from the POS plus 3 months of spend baseline | A pilot group gives us a POS export and confirms it would pay extra for this. |
| 1 | Unified order placement | No evidence buyers want it (C1, C3, C6, C7), and suppliers won't pay for it (C5, C8) | 3 of our first 10 paying customers ask to order through us, **and** talks with 3 customers of the Finnish competitor show suppliers there really pay per order. |
| 2 | Supplier catalogue with pricing | Only exists to serve #1. Prices are negotiated per customer (C2), so a shared catalogue is wrong by default. | #1 is promoted. Until then, the invoices *are* the price list. |

## Never

| # | Item | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | Native mobile app for chefs | The buyer is finance, not the chef. Anything the kitchen touches has to need no install (WhatsApp photo, mobile web). An app store download is friction we'd pay for and get nothing back. |
| 10 | Supplier ratings and reviews | Estonia has about 1,400 venues, and supplier relationships are personal ("Jaan knows me"). Public ratings would turn the suppliers we need credits from into enemies. Nobody asked for it. Private supplier scorecards for the customer are part of #5, not this. |
| 11 | WhatsApp *ordering* bot | It automates the part buyers say already works. WhatsApp stays, but as the channel for delivery-note photos, not for orders. |
| 13 | Par levels and stock tracking | A separate product category with its own incumbents. It needs daily counts kitchens don't do, and it serves a different user (the chef). It would pull us away from the recovery wedge. |
| 14 | Self-service supplier onboarding portal | Our model needs **zero** supplier sign-up. Credit requests go by email. Suppliers have shown they won't adopt portals (C5: 15% uptake) or integrate (C8: a system from 2009). |
